# Decision-Making Policy

Status: Adopted

## Default method

Decisions are made by the accountable team after seeking input from materially affected contributors. Consensus is preferred, but lack of unanimity does not create an indefinite veto.

## Decision levels

### Routine

Reversible implementation and maintenance choices are made in pull requests, issues, or maintainer discussion. No separate record is required beyond the work itself.

### Significant

Architecture, compatibility, repository lifecycle, security posture, governance, licensing, or long-lived operational commitments require a durable decision record. The record states context, options, decision, accountable owner, consequences, and review date if temporary.

### Emergency

An owner, security maintainer, or project maintainer may take the minimum action needed to contain active compromise, abuse, legal exposure, or service harm. Emergency action must be documented and reviewed promptly after containment.

## Objections and escalation

An objection should identify a concrete security, legal, technical, community, or sustainability risk and propose a condition that would resolve it.

Escalation order:

1. accountable project or functional team;
2. governance for cross-project authority or process disputes;
3. organization owners for platform, legal-entity, or emergency matters that cannot be delegated.

Security and legal-compliance may block a release within their defined scope. The block and the evidence needed to clear it must be recorded. They do not gain general roadmap authority.

## Conflicts of interest

A person discloses a material financial, employment, family, competitive, or personal interest before participating in a decision. They may provide factual context but should not be the sole approver. When recusal would leave no qualified decision maker, the conflict and the reason for proceeding are recorded.

## Future council

At Stage 2, a governance council should have three to seven members, published terms, staggered appointments where practical, conflict rules, removal procedure, meeting records, and an explicit list of reserved powers. The council should delegate project decisions and use its broad authority rarely.

# Funding and Conflicts Policy

Status: Adopted baseline

## Principles

Funding must not silently purchase technical authority, moderation outcomes, security exceptions, or privileged access. Public claims about sponsorship, independence, and use of funds must remain accurate.

## Current-stage controls

- Public funding pages describe what support enables without promising unavailable support or influence.
- Donor and transaction data is limited to people who need it for administration, accounting, fraud response, or legal obligations.
- Credentials for payment platforms are protected with strong authentication and recovery material.
- Material donations, sponsorship conditions, refunds, chargebacks, and platform restrictions are recorded outside public repositories.
- A person with a material financial interest discloses it before related organization decisions.

## Restricted arrangements

Greyfoundry must not accept funding that requires undisclosed promotion, hidden technical control, weakened security, discriminatory access, deceptive claims, or suppression of vulnerability information.

Restricted funds are used only for the stated purpose. If the condition cannot be met, the funds should be declined or returned where legally and operationally possible.

## Stage 3 controls

When funding becomes material or recurring:

- publish a budget authority and spending policy;
- separate approval, payment execution, and reconciliation between independent people where possible;
- record contracts, tax obligations, beneficial interests, and ownership of purchased assets;
- publish an annual high-level transparency report that protects personal donor data;
- define reimbursement, compensation, and related-party transaction rules;
- retain financial records for the legally required period;
- decide which legal entity owns accounts, trademarks, domains, and funded work.

## Project independence

Sponsors may fund defined work and provide requirements, but project maintainers retain technical judgment within the agreed scope and organization policy. Sponsored work is identified when the relationship would be material to a reasonable contributor's understanding.
